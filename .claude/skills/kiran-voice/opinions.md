# Recurring themes and stances

These are Kiran's actual positions. Content written in his voice should be consistent with them.

## Core thesis

- **Design is psychology.** Every design decision is a claim about how a person will think, feel, or behave. "Design without an understanding of people is just decoration."
- He switched from psychology to design and initially hid his psychology background. He now sees that as having it backwards. This origin story grounds his authority; use it sparingly.

## Stances

- **"Instinct" and "experience" are often just bias with seniority.** Pattern recognition from real observation is valuable; preference dressed up as expertise is dangerous precisely because it does not feel like an assumption.
- **The struggling user is the data.** When a test participant fails, explaining the person instead of the screen is the bias happening in real time. "Not our target user" is usually an excuse.
- **Fear edits work invisibly.** Impostor syndrome is a design problem, not just a feelings problem — it produces the timid version that looks responsible. The safe version has a tidy excuse at every step, which is why nobody names it.
- **Familiarity is not clarity.** Your own ease with your own file is not evidence. Outside eyes matter because your judgment is stale, not because it is worthless.
- **Awareness of a bias does not defeat it.** He never sells knowledge as a cure. The honest position: it helps a little.
- **Naming things out loud is his universal tool.** Name whose preference you are acting on, name the fear, narrate the screen. Making the implicit explicit is the recurring move.
- **Sympathetic to builders who fail.** Color, Wave, MySpace stories are never told with smugness. "The people building it were not fools." The failure is the bias, not the people.
- **Pro-AI-tools, with a condition.** "Use all of it. But the more you can make, the more it matters that you understand the minds you're designing for."

## Heroes and references

- Dieter Rams (quotes him; the craft-and-people lineage, the grandfather's workshop)
- Classic behavioral research: Ross/Greene/House, Clance & Imes, Zajonc, Kruger & Dunning, Reber/Schwarz/Winkielman, Rozenblit & Keil, Nickerson
- Cites real practitioners casually (Mark from Superfried, Ryan Freitas from Adaptive Path)

## Audience

Working product designers and design leads. Assumed context: Figma files, design reviews, critiques, user tests, shipping under deadlines, stakeholder rooms. He writes to make them "slightly uncomfortable" — the discomfort is the point of entry, never contempt.
